## Work

For a constant force:

$$W = Fd\cos\theta$$

### Cases

| Case | Angle $\theta$ | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Force along motion | $0^\circ$ | positive work |
| Force opposite motion | $180^\circ$ | negative work |
| Force perpendicular to motion | $90^\circ$ | zero work |

### Work-Energy Theorem

$$W_{\text{net}} = \Delta K$$

---

## Kinetic and Potential Energy

$$K = \frac12 mv^2$$
$$U_g = mgh$$
$$U_s = \frac12 kx^2$$

### When to Use Each

| Energy Type | Use When |
|---|---|
| Kinetic | object has speed |
| Gravitational potential | height changes near Earth's surface |
| Spring potential | spring stretches or compresses |

---

## Mechanical Energy Conservation

If only conservative forces act:

$$K_i + U_i = K_f + U_f$$

If non-conservative work is present:

$$K_i + U_i + W_{nc} = K_f + U_f$$

### Common Cases

| Situation | Preferred Method |
|---|---|
| speed-height conversion | energy conservation |
| spring compression to speed | spring energy to kinetic |
| friction included | energy with $W_{nc}$ |
| many forces but few positions | energy often simpler than Newton's laws |

---

## Power

$$P = \frac{W}{t}, \qquad P = Fv\cos\theta$$

Power is the rate of doing work or transferring energy.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1 — Work by Constant Force
A $50\,\text{N}$ force moves a box $10\,\text{m}$ horizontally.

$$W = 50(10) = 500\,\text{J}$$

### Example 2 — Work at an Angle
A $100\,\text{N}$ force acts at $60^\circ$ over $5\,\text{m}$.

$$W = 100(5)\cos 60^\circ = 250\,\text{J}$$

### Example 3 — Kinetic Energy Change
A $2\,\text{kg}$ object speeds up from $5$ to $10\,\text{m/s}$.

$$\Delta K = \frac12 (2)(10^2-5^2) = 75\,\text{J}$$

### Example 4 — Dropped Ball
A ball falls from $10\,\text{m}$, starting from rest, with $g=10\,\text{m/s}^2$.

$$mgh = \frac12 mv^2 \Rightarrow 10(10) = \frac12 v^2$$
$$v = \sqrt{200} \approx 14.1\,\text{m/s}$$

### Example 5 — Spring Compression
A spring with $k=200\,\text{N/m}$ is compressed by $0.30\,\text{m}$.

$$U_s = \frac12 (200)(0.30)^2 = 9\,\text{J}$$

### Example 6 — Power
A worker lifts a $50\,\text{kg}$ box by $2\,\text{m}$ in $4\,\text{s}$.

$$W = mgh = 50(10)(2)=1000\,\text{J}$$
$$P = \frac{1000}{4} = 250\,\text{W}$$

---

## Strategy Notes
- Use energy when you care about initial and final states more than intermediate forces.
- Use work by friction as a negative term.
- If mass cancels, do not keep carrying it through every step.
